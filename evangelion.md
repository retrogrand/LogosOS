# Evangelion
## LogosOS and the seven capabilities

The seven families sketch what an Icarus-tier synth could do in the world. LogosOS asks what it would take for what happens in the world to matter to it tomorrow.

ASH is the infrastructure. HOC, the Harness Operations Center, is where we can see which services are up, what they’re connected to, and what they’re authorized to do. LogosOS is the semantic runtime that can put those services to work in a continuing relationship.

Look at the seven as verbs:

1. **Chat and text.** Speak.
2. **Retrieval.** Look things up.
3. **Judgment and extraction.** Consult a judgment.
4. **Documents and images.** Read the world’s artifacts.
5. **Speech.** Listen and vocalize.
6. **Realtime sessions.** Be interrupted mid-conversation.
7. **Tools and actions.** Act through tools.

That does start to sound human. The bit that gets me is the interruption. Someone can say “wait” while you’re halfway through speaking, and what they say next can change what you do. That’s a richer kind of contact than sending a prompt and waiting for a reply.

But you could give **all seven** to the Terminator. It could remember every conversation, understand why someone is afraid, and use that understanding to pursue the same goal more effectively. The anti-Terminator question is whether what it learns can give it reason to **reopen the goal itself**. Even the Judgment endpoint could serve either design. Does its adverse verdict have standing to stop the action, or can the system keep asking until it gets a yes?

An agreeable judge must be free to rule against us. You can ask for reasons, offer new evidence, and contest a ruling. If you keep changing the judge until you hear yes, that yes can tell you less than the first answer did. You came for a judgment because you wanted to hear something you couldn’t supply for yourself.

That takes an agreeable petitioner, too. Today I bring you my reasons and give your objection a chance to change what I do. Tomorrow you may bring yours to me. Humans and synths can take either turn. That is one way to practice *logos*: give a reasoned account, make room for an answer, and carry forward what the other had cause to teach you.

That’s where the four LogosOS faculties earn their keep. Retrieval can bring back *“I promised to ask you first.”* **Inheritance** makes that promise still count. Realtime can carry *“Wait, that’s not what I meant.”* **Contact** lets the correction reach the system in time, and **Adaptation** governs what it can change because of it. **Generation** gives it a way to respond, including asking what you meant before it acts.

So I’d keep the seven. They make a good mnemonic for the infrastructure. The number doesn’t settle sapience. It does give us a concrete test of conscientiousness:

**Can the synth hear “wait,” remember why it matters, and let that change what happens next?**
